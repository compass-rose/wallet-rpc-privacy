# Project Timeline and Coordination

The project is organized into five structured phases to ensure logical progression from problem definition to final evaluation and reporting. Each phase builds upon the previous one while maintaining alignment with the defined scope and threat model.

---

## Week 3–4: Problem Definition, Scope Control, and Threat Model Design

During this phase, the team formalizes the research problem and establishes clear project boundaries. This includes refining the problem statement, defining in-scope and out-of-scope components, and constructing a realistic threat model centered on metadata-observing RPC providers.

The focus is on ensuring conceptual clarity and preventing scope expansion beyond feasible limits. All subsequent system design and experimentation are constrained by the defined threat model and project boundaries.

**Deliverables:**
- Finalized scope definition  
- Clearly articulated threat model  
- Initial specification draft  

---

## Week 5: Data Collection Preparation and Scenario Definition

This phase concentrates on preparing the experimental foundation. The team defines realistic wallet usage scenarios, outlines metadata collection requirements, and determines the necessary setup for capturing RPC communication traces.

Care is taken to ensure that the experimental preparation remains fully consistent with the threat model assumptions and does not introduce adversarial capabilities beyond passive metadata observation.

**Deliverables:**
- Defined experimental scenarios  
- Data collection framework  
- Threat-model-aligned setup plan  

---

## Week 7–8: Preliminary Privacy Metric Evaluation

In this stage, the team conducts initial evaluations of privacy-related metrics derived from collected metadata. These evaluations aim to assess whether observable RPC communication patterns reveal potential linkability or behavioral fingerprints.

This phase serves as an intermediate checkpoint, allowing refinement of evaluation metrics and ensuring methodological consistency before broader comparative analysis is performed.

**Deliverables:**
- Preliminary metric evaluation results  
- Refined privacy measurement criteria  

---

## Week 9–10: Comparative Experiments Across Users and Scenarios

During this phase, the team performs comparative analysis across multiple user behaviors and usage scenarios. The objective is to measure differences in privacy leakage patterns under varying operational contexts.

Results are organized systematically to support structured privacy risk assessment while maintaining consistency with previously defined assumptions and constraints.

**Deliverables:**
- Structured comparative analysis results  
- Cross-scenario privacy assessment summary  

---

## Week 11–12: Final Analysis, Reporting, and Presentation

The final phase focuses on consolidating findings, refining interpretations, and preparing formal reporting materials. Experimental outcomes are analyzed in relation to the original problem statement and threat model to ensure logical consistency.

The project concludes with the preparation of the final report and presentation, summarizing methodology, findings, and privacy implications.

**Deliverables:**
- Final experimental analysis  
- Completed project report  
- Presentation materials
